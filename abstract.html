Abstract: Days of Futures Past

1984 wasn't like 1984

In the mid-1980s, artist Mike Saenz and writer Peter B. Gillis created the comic book Shatter on the Apple Macintosh, which had been introduced only in 1984. It was the very first comic book drawn on a computer with a mouse, at the time a new kind of input device.

Figure 1: Shatter, the first comic book created on a computer in 1984

The production process established in Shatter was a harbinger of things to come, and it also showed which parts of that process would prove to be dead ends. Unusually for the time, Peter B. Gillis wrote the scripts on a computer and sent them to Mike Saenz. Saenz painstakingly drew the comic panels on the Apple Macintosh and placed lettering and sound effects directly in the paint software. The finished pages were printed and then colored by traditional means.

The Roots of the Art Form

In 2018, commercial comic books are drawn using a wide variety of techniques. Most are still drawn by hand in the traditional process of penciling and inking, but the pages are then scanned so that colors, lettering and sound effects can be added digitally.

In this paper, we examine the history of Shatter, the subjects it covers, and the tools used to create its unique art style. We contrast the way commercial comic books were created in 1985 with the way Shatter was made. Finally, we trace the path from the process used for Shatter to the one used for modern-day commercial comics.
